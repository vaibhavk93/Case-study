How to use this workbook

Six rules. They are the difference between practice that compounds and practice that plateaus.

  1. Set a timer for the stated length. Cases expand to fill available time; a timer is the only thing that teaches pacing.
  2. Speak out loud. Not in your head. Your head is fluent and forgiving; your mouth is not. Record yourself on a phone if nobody is available.
  3. Write your structure before you say anything about solutions. If you cannot draw it, you do not have it. That is what the ruled space below each prompt is for.
  4. Give a real recommendation with a number attached, even if the number is invented — as long as you say it is invented.
  5. Only then open the solution in the interactive guide, and read it looking for what you did not consider, not for whether you "got it right".
  6. Fill in the journal entry. Three lines. This is the step everybody skips and it is where nearly all the improvement comes from.

The comfort trap

It is pleasant to practise the case types you are already good at, and your weak categories will quietly go unpractised for months. Use the tracker below: if you have nine product cases ticked and zero profitability cases, that is your answer about what to do next.

The practice journal

Three minutes per case. This is the step that converts practice into improvement, and it is the one everybody skips.

Date:              ____________________
Case:              ____________________  (id + one-line prompt)
Type / level:      ____________________
Time taken:        _______ (target _______)
Target dimension:  ____________________  (one, from the Ch 33 rubric)

WHAT I MISSED
  ____________________________________________________________

WHY I MISSED IT
  ____________________________________________________________
  (a habit, a gap in knowledge, or time pressure — be specific)

WHAT I'LL DO DIFFERENTLY NEXT TIME
  ____________________________________________________________
  (one concrete change, phrased as a trigger: "when X, I will Y")

SELF-SCORE (1-4)
  Framing ___  Structure ___  Analysis ___  Root cause ___
  Prioritisation ___  Business impact ___  Metrics ___  Close ___

Reread the last three entries before every session. After ten entries, read them all at once: your two habitual failure modes will be unmistakable, and they will be the same two every time. Work on those for a fortnight and ignore everything else.

The trigger format

"I'll be more structured" never changes anything. "When I'm handed a metric drop, I will ask whether the measurement changed before I say anything about causes" does, because it names the situation and the action. Write every improvement note that way.

The three-question shortcut, if a full rubric is too heavy for routine practice: did I diagnose before solving? did I prioritise explicitly, with criteria? did I close with a recommendation, a metric and a risk? Three yeses is a level-3 performance.

Isolation drills

Full cases are an inefficient way to fix a specific weakness. These take 10–15 minutes each and target one dimension. Pick the one matching your lowest rubric score.

TargetDrillReps
StructuringRead 10 prompts from the lab; structure each in 90 seconds; never solve them10
ClarifyingFor 10 prompts write 3 questions, each with "the reason I'm asking is…"10
Arithmetic20 percentage-change and funnel-multiplication problems, no calculatorDaily
SizingSize one thing a day, two ways, in five minutesDaily
SegmentationPick a product; write 3 behavioural segments and the different feature each needs10
MetricsFor 15 features: primary metric, input metric, guardrail15
ClosingRead a solved case; deliver only the 60-second close, recorded10
Second-orderFor 10 recommendations, write what happens after it works10

The structuring drill is the highest-return exercise available. Ten prompts, structure only, no solving — because structure is a motor skill and solving contaminates the repetition.

The retrieval deck

Cut these out, or fold the page so the answers are hidden. Answer out loud before you look — recognising an answer is not the same as producing one, and only the second predicts performance under pressure.

The interactive guide runs this deck on an expanding schedule (same day → 1 day → 3 days → 1 week → 3 weeks). On paper, mark each card ✓ or ✗ and re-test the ✗ cards the next day.

The spine · 8 cards

QuestionAnswerGot it
The five words of the spine, in order?WHAT · WHERE · WHY · WHICH · SO WHAT. Recognise → cut → find the cause → choose → land it.☐
WHAT expands into which three steps?Frame → Objective → Scope. Restate it, establish what success means, then say what is in and out.☐
WHERE expands into what?The four cuts (Formula, Flow, Family, Fork) and the three tests (Sum, Orphan, Altitude).☐
WHY expands into which six steps?REAL → SIZE → WHERE → WHICH → WHY → NEXT. Never reorder them — SIZE and WHICH are the two most often skipped.☐
WHICH expands into what?Criteria → Gates → Score → Sequence. Criteria before scoring; gates are not options.☐
SO WHAT expands into which six elements?Recommendation · Why · Impact · Metric · Risk · Monday.☐
Why question words rather than an acronym?Acronyms optimise recall and sacrifice fit. These are the questions you would ask anyway, in their natural order — a hook that does not bend the problem to the label.☐
The two cuts for recognising a case?Domain (what it is about — the ten families) and output (what you must hand back — design, measure, diagnose, experiment, prioritise, strategise, estimate, system design, narrate). They are independent.☐

MECE · 17 cards

QuestionAnswerGot it
What does MECE stand for, and what does each half prevent?Mutually Exclusive — no overlaps, which protects your arithmetic. Collectively Exhaustive — no gaps, which stops the answer hiding outside your tree.☐
Which MECE failure is worse, and why?The gap. An overlap corrupts arithmetic, which is visible. A gap hides the answer silently — nothing in your structure points at what you left out.☐
Name the four MECE cut types.Formula (arithmetic) · Flow (sequence) · Family (categories) · Fork (X vs not-X).☐
Which cut is MECE by construction, and why does that matter?Fork — X vs not-X. It is a logical guarantee, not a heuristic, so it cannot fail. Reach for it when you are unsure and under time pressure.☐
What is the Sum Test?Do the branches add back to the whole? Summing to 112% means an overlap; 86% means a gap.☐
What is the Orphan Test?Name one awkward edge case and ask which branch it belongs to. None = gap. Two = overlap. Exactly one = the cut survives.☐
Why is the Orphan Test better than asking 'is my tree MECE?''Is it MECE?' is unanswerable under pressure. 'Where does click-and-collect go?' is answerable in two seconds and catches most real failures.☐
What is the practical payoff of a mutually exclusive cut?One number can kill a whole branch permanently. In a non-MECE tree, eliminating a branch proves nothing, because the thing you killed might also sit in the branch you kept.☐
Which branch do people forget when splitting a market?Non-consumption — people who buy from nobody. 'Our share + competitors' share' is not exhaustive, and the opportunity often lives in the missing branch.☐
Can a structure be perfectly MECE and still useless?Yes. MECE guarantees no gaps; it does not guarantee relevance. Splitting by hair colour is MECE. You also need an axis on which branches behave differently.☐
How do you declare a gap without being penalised?Say it: 'That's not perfectly exhaustive — I've left out X because it's second-order here.' A declared gap is a scoping decision, not an error.☐
MECE in a consulting case vs a PM case?Consulting: explicit, visible, probed with 'what else?'. PM: run it privately, spend visible time on depth. A rigid MECE tree in a product case reads as mechanical.☐
What is the most reliable source of a MECE split?A mathematical identity. Revenue = Customers × Frequency × Revenue per transaction is MECE by construction — the terms multiply to exactly the metric, so no overlap and no gap are possible.☐
The prompt contains a number. What do you do before saying any idea?Write its identity. You cannot freeze while writing an equation, and by the time it is on the page your buckets already exist — derived, not invented.☐
Name the four structure generators and when each fires.Equation (a metric or ratio) · Funnel/journey (a behaviour or drop-off) · Entity/actor (a marketplace or platform) · Lifecycle (retention, cohorts, growth).☐
What does the Altitude Test catch?Mixed levels of abstraction. "Pricing strategy" beside "button placement" — they do not overlap, but one belongs a level down. It signals a cut assembled from whatever came to mind.☐
Three conditions for stopping decomposition?The diagnosis is actionable · ownership shifts to another function · no new insight is appearing. Any one is enough.☐

Diagnosis · 13 cards

QuestionAnswerGot it
What do you check before hypothesising about any metric drop?Is it real? Tracking change, definition change, seasonality, data completeness, and whether the move exceeds normal volatility. Cheapest check, commonest cause of a cliff.☐
A cliff on one date, uniform across every segment — most likely cause?A measurement change. Real behavioural causes almost always show segment variation.☐
A cliff on one date confined to one platform?A release on that platform.☐
A gradual slide across all segments?Structural — competition, market, pricing, mix or seasonality. These are the hard ones; a cliff is a gift.☐
In what order do you search for causes?Internal → external → the world. Ours (releases, prices, policy, supply), then theirs (competitor), then the world (season, macro, regulation). Internal is cheaper to check and more often right.☐
The four tests before you believe a cause?Timing · Dose–response · Magnitude · Counterfactual.☐
Which validation test kills the most bad theories?Magnitude. A cause affecting 2% of users cannot explain a 20% drop.☐
Your causes explain 7 of a 15-point drop. What does that mean?There is another cause. Attribution that does not sum to the gap is incomplete — say so and name the residual rather than forcing a fit.☐
Three alternatives to a causal claim like 'users who do X retain better'?Selection (they were already committed), reverse causation (retention causes X), confounding (both driven by use case). Name the specific one; reciting 'correlation isn't causation' is worthless.☐
Aggregate improved but every segment got worse. What happened?Simpson's paradox — a mix shift. Never accept an aggregate movement without asking whether the composition changed.☐
The six-step diagnostic, in order?REAL → SIZE → WHERE → WHICH → WHY → NEXT. SIZE and WHICH are the two most often skipped.☐
What does SIZE add that validity does not?How big, how sudden, how unusual against normal volatility. A 15% move on a metric that routinely swings 12% may be nothing.☐
What does WHICH mean, and why does skipping it hurt?Decompose the metric to its identity and find the term that moved. Skip it and you hunt for causes of a change you have not located — which is how a revenue fall that was entirely mix gets explained as demand.☐

Structure & numbers · 15 cards

QuestionAnswerGot it
Two rules for building a tree under time pressure?One split, with a reason — not four candidates. Two levels deep, not four — depth beyond your evidence is fiction.☐
What is the first cut for any revenue question?Revenue = Volume × Price — then check mix, which is the most-missed effect.☐
When reading a funnel, what must you report alongside the rate?Absolute volume lost. The worst rate is often not the biggest opportunity.☐
What single number reframes a commerce case?Contribution margin per unit. If negative, growth makes the loss bigger — so growth is the problem, not the strategy.☐
What does a flattening retention curve tell you?That a group exists for whom the product genuinely works. A curve decaying to zero means no product-market fit, however good week one looks.☐
Rule of 72?Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ growth rate. 12% → 6 years; 18% → 4 years.☐
A metric falls 20%. What rise restores it?+25%, not +20%. 100 → 80 → 100 requires a quarter back on. Falling by 1/5 needs a rise of 1/4.☐
Which estimation term do candidates forget on the supply side?Utilisation / duty cycle. A charger, phlebotomist or agent is not productive 24 hours a day.☐
How do you close a market sizing answer?Range, the dominant assumption, and the implication for the decision. Almost everyone stops at the number.☐
Why prefer CAC payback over LTV:CAC?LTV compounds three uncertain estimates; payback uses two. Harder to fool yourself with.☐
Three trees — what is each for?Issue: what could be causing this (diagnosis, neutral). Hypothesis: why I believe this branch (investigation, directional). Solution: what we do about the confirmed cause (synthesis, then ranked).☐
In what order do you test hypotheses?By probability × testability — what is both likely and cheap to check, not what you are most attached to. And say what data would kill each one.☐
Universal revenue identity?Revenue = Customers × Frequency × Revenue per transaction☐
Advertising revenue identity?Revenue = DAU × Sessions per DAU × Ads per session × CPM ÷ 1000☐
Marketplace revenue identity?Revenue = GMV × Take rate, where Transactions = Supply × Utilisation × Bookings per unit☐

Judgement & delivery · 14 cards

QuestionAnswerGot it
What must be separated before any prioritisation?Gates from scored items. Legal, contractual, security and table-stakes happen regardless. Scoring a gate against value items is a category error.☐
When should you say criteria?Before scoring. Criteria stated afterwards look reverse-engineered, because they usually are.☐
Model and instinct disagree on a ranking. What do you do?Find the factor the model is missing — usually compounding or strategic fit — name it, and decide openly. Do not quietly re-weight until it agrees.☐
What percentage of capacity should a quarter plan use?About 60–75%. A plan with no slack is a plan that fails.☐
The sentence that buys you thinking time?'Let me take twenty seconds to organise my thoughts.' Then actually be silent.☐
The highest-value phrase when asking a clarifying question?'The reason I'm asking is…' — it converts information-gathering into visible hypothesis-driven thinking.☐
How do you respond to a hint?Acknowledge → engage genuinely → adjust, or defend once with a reason → move on. Defending after being handed evidence is the fastest way to lose a case you were winning.☐
The six elements of a close?Recommendation · Why · Expected impact · Metric and guardrail · Biggest risk · Monday.☐
The sentence almost nobody says?'What would change my mind is [the assumption carrying the weight].'☐
What is the meta-failure behind most case mistakes?Avoiding commitment. A confident, revisable wrong answer beats a hedged non-answer — being wrong is recoverable in the conversation; being undecidable is not.☐
Interviewer-led vs candidate-led — what changes?Interviewer-led: answer this question completely, then stop; do not run ahead. Candidate-led: drive, and say where you are going and why.☐
What should you ask if you do not know which format you are in?'Would you like me to drive the analysis, or would you prefer to steer me through it?'☐
Guardrail versus counter metric?A guardrail is what must not get worse. A counter metric moves in the opposite direction if your change is working for the wrong reason — e.g. ad load up, time spent down.☐
Counter metric for simplifying onboarding?Week-4 retention. You may simply be activating people who were never going to stay.☐

Product & AI · 8 cards

QuestionAnswerGot it
The test for whether a segmentation is any good?If two segments would get the same feature, they are one segment. Behaviour and frequency beat demographics.☐
Three questions at each journey stage?What must they do, know, and trust? Most friction is in the last two; most product work addresses only the first.☐
The problem statement template?User segment [X] experiences [problem] while [context], resulting in [impact], because [root cause].☐
What decides how much human review an AI feature needs?The cost of an error. Cheap and reversible: automate. Expensive and silent: draft-and-approve. Catastrophic: do not use a model.☐
The metric to run an assistive AI product on?Acceptance rate without edits — directly observable, maps to value, and degrades visibly when quality drops.☐
Why must AI evaluation be sliced by segment?An aggregate accuracy number hides total failure on a subgroup — a different language, document type or device.☐
What do candidates forget in AI product cases?Inference cost as a share of revenue per user. Unlike classical software, AI features have real marginal cost.☐
The one marketplace metric that matters?Liquidity — the probability a request is matched quickly. Not GMV, not supplier count.☐

Consulting cases · 8 cards

QuestionAnswerGot it
The profitability tree, top two levels?Profit = Revenue − Cost; Revenue = Price × Volume; Cost = Fixed + Variable.☐
What must an M&A answer end with?A clear yes or no, against a bar you stated before analysing.☐
The four branches of an M&A case?Why buy (the buyer's goal) · Is the target worth it (standalone value) · Synergies and fit · Risks and alternatives.☐
What kills most market entry answers?Describing the market and never deciding. State the bar for 'yes' before you analyse.☐
What do you do after every exhibit?Say the 'so what' — one sentence of insight, then what it changes about your hypothesis.☐
How do you structure a brainstorming answer?Take ten seconds, give a MECE structure first, then populate it. Structured beats longer: six ideas in two buckets beats twelve at random.☐
Most entry failures are failures of what?Capability, not market. The question is rarely 'is this market attractive?' but 'are we the right company to win it?'☐
What is the first move in a cost-reduction case?Split fixed vs variable, then rank by size — and ask which costs are driving revenue before cutting them.☐
